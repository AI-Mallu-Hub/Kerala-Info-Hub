🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define nervous tissue.
  • Identify the two major cell types in nervous tissue.
  • Describe the basic structure of a neuron.
  • Identify the main parts of a neuron.
  • Explain the general direction of information flow through a neuron.
  • Recognize the major structural types of neurons.
  • Identify the major glial cells of the CNS and PNS.
  • Explain the basic role of myelin.

📖 Introduction

The nervous system allows the body to receive information, process it, and produce appropriate responses.


Nervous tissue is found in both the central nervous system (CNS) and the peripheral nervous system (PNS).


It contains two major types of cells: neurons and glial cells.

🔬 Nervous Tissue Overview

Neurons + Glial cells

📌 Simple Idea

Nervous tissue → Neurons + Glial cells

🧠 What is Nervous Tissue?

Nervous tissue is specialized tissue that can receive, process, and communicate information.


Neurons are electrically active cells that communicate information through electrical and chemical signals.


Glial cells support neurons and help maintain the environment in which neurons function.

🔬 Neuron + Glial Cells

The two major cell classes of nervous tissue

⚡ Neurons

Neurons are specialized cells that communicate information within the nervous system.


A neuron has a cell body and extensions called processes.

The major processes are dendrites and an axon.

📌 Remember

Neuron = Cell body + Dendrites + Axon

🔬 Structure of a Neuron

The structure of a neuron is closely related to its function.


1. Cell Body / Soma

The cell body, also called the soma, contains the nucleus and most of the major organelles.


2. Dendrites

Dendrites usually receive information from other neurons.


3. Axon

The axon carries signals away from the cell body toward target cells.

🔬 Structure of a Neuron

Soma, nucleus, dendrites, axon and axon terminals

➡️ Direction of Information Flow

In the basic model of a neuron, information generally flows from the dendrites toward the cell body and then along the axon toward its target.


This organization gives the neuron a structural and functional polarity.

Planned visual: Dendrites → Soma → Axon → Target

📌 Remember

Receive → Process → Send

🧩 Types of Neurons

Neurons can be classified according to the number and arrangement of their processes.


1. Unipolar

A single process is associated with the cell body.


2. Bipolar

Two processes extend from the cell body.


3. Multipolar

Multiple processes extend from the cell body.

🛡️ Glial Cells

Glial cells, also called neuroglia or glia, are the other major cell type in nervous tissue.


They perform several support and maintenance functions that help neurons work properly.

🛡️ Glial Cell Overview

Planned visual: Major CNS and PNS glial cells

📌 Simple Idea

Glia → Support + Maintain + Protect

🧠 Glial Cells of the CNS

The central nervous system contains four major types of glial cells.

  • Astrocytes – support neurons and help maintain the extracellular environment.
  • Oligodendrocytes – form myelin around axons in the CNS.
  • Microglia – provide immune surveillance and phagocytic functions.
  • Ependymal cells – line the ventricles and are involved in cerebrospinal fluid production.

🔗 Glial Cells of the PNS

The peripheral nervous system contains two major types of glial cells.

  • Schwann cells – form myelin around peripheral axons.
  • Satellite cells – support neuron cell bodies in peripheral ganglia.

⚡ Myelin

Myelin is a lipid-rich sheath that surrounds many axons.


It helps insulate the axon and facilitates the transmission of electrical signals.


In the CNS, myelin is formed by oligodendrocytes.

In the PNS, myelin is formed by Schwann cells.

📌 Remember

🏥 Clinical Correlation

Changes or injury involving nervous tissue can affect sensation, movement, coordination, or other nervous system functions.


⚡ Peripheral Nerve Injury

Damage to peripheral nerves may cause symptoms such as weakness, altered sensation, or pain.


🧠 Demyelination

Damage to myelin can interfere with normal signal transmission along axons.

💡 Clinical Point

The structure of neurons and their supporting cells is closely related to how the nervous system communicates.
