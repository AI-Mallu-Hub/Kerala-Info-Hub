📖 Introduction

Every living organism, from a tiny bacterium to the human body, is made up of cells. The human body contains trillions of cells working together to form tissues, organs and organ systems.


Cells perform essential functions such as obtaining nutrients, producing energy, removing waste products, growing, reproducing and maintaining life.


Understanding the cell is the foundation of Anatomy, Physiology, Pathology, Pharmacology and Nursing Science.

🧫 What is a Cell?

A cell is the smallest structural and functional unit of life.


It is called the building block of the human body because every tissue and organ is made up of cells.

📌 Definition

Cell = Structural + Functional Unit of Life.

🧬 Cell Theory

The Cell Theory was proposed by Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann, and later expanded by Rudolf Virchow.


Main Principles

  • All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • All new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

💡 Exam Tip

The statement "All cells arise from pre-existing cells" was introduced by Rudolf Virchow and is frequently asked in Nursing entrance examinations.

🔬 Types of Cells

Based on their internal structure, cells are classified into two major types.

Type Characteristics
Prokaryotic Cell Simple cell without a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
Eukaryotic Cell Complex cell with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

📊 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

Feature Prokaryotic Eukaryotic
Nucleus Absent Present
DNA Circular Linear
Organelles Absent Present
Cell Size Smaller Larger
Examples Bacteria Human, Animal & Plant Cells

⚙️ Cell Organelles

Cell organelles are specialized structures inside the cell. Each organelle performs a specific function necessary for survival.

Organelle Main Function
🧠 Nucleus Controls all cell activities and stores DNA.
🏭 Ribosomes Protein synthesis.
🛣️ Endoplasmic Reticulum Transport of proteins and lipids.
📦 Golgi Apparatus Packages and secretes proteins.
⚡ Mitochondria Produces ATP (energy).
🗑️ Lysosome Digests waste materials.
💡 Remember: Every organelle has a unique job. Together they keep the cell alive.

💡 Exam Tip

Questions comparing Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic cells are frequently asked in PSC, MOHAP, DOH and DHA examinations.

🔋 Cell Functions

Although cells vary in size and shape, all living cells perform a common set of essential functions.

  • ⚡ Produce energy (ATP)
  • 🍽️ Absorb nutrients
  • 🧬 Synthesize proteins
  • 🚚 Transport substances
  • 🗑️ Remove waste products
  • 📈 Grow and divide
  • 🛡️ Maintain homeostasis
  • 📨 Respond to external stimuli
🏥 Clinical Correlation Mitochondrial diseases reduce ATP production, causing muscle weakness and neurological problems.

🧠 Memory Trick

Pro = Primitive

Eu = True

👉 Prokaryotic = Primitive Cell
👉 Eukaryotic = True Nucleus
